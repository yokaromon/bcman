# Retaking a card overwrites the same Business Card

A user can retake a single Business Card's photograph to replace a blurred or badly cropped image. The retaken image replaces the corrected and orientation-corrected images of the *same* Business Card record; `photo_id` keeps pointing at the Photo the card was originally detected in, and the retaken source photo is never stored as a Photo of its own.

Two alternatives were rejected. Storing the retake as a new Photo and moving the Business Card onto it keeps provenance exact, but the old Photo silently loses a card while a one-card Photo appears in the list — the photo-by-photo view becomes littered with retakes that were never really separate captures. Creating a fresh Photo, Business Card and Contact and deleting the old ones is the most correct model, but it reaches into Touch History and merge decisions in the Directory context for what the user experiences as "this picture is blurry".

The cost is that `corrected_image_path` no longer derives from the region of the Photo that `photo_id`, `x/y/width/height` and `detection_confidence` describe. Those detection fields and `detected.jpg` are deliberately left untouched, because they remain true statements about the original Photo — only the corrected image ceases to be derived from it. The replacement is recorded in `ProcessingHistory` as `image_replacement`, which is the only place the substitution is visible after the fact.

Previous image generations are not retained: applying a retake deletes the superseded `oriented-*.jpg` and their thumbnails. This is a deliberate exception to ADR 0007's principle that card evidence survives the purge of its source Photo — a user who retakes badly, on a card whose Photo was already purged, cannot recover the earlier crop. It was accepted because the user approves the cropped result in a preview before anything is overwritten, and because keeping generations would require retention and cleanup rules that this PoC does not otherwise have.
